# SIP vs Lumpsum (and SWP/STP)

**What it is:** Nearly every fund is open-ended: units are created/extinguished at that day's NAV (~3pm cutoff). Lumpsum is a one-shot investment exposed to entry-date luck. SIP is a fixed amount on a fixed date, monthly.

**How to read it:** SIP isn't magic math — it's automated, emotion-free averaging of entry points. It matters because the biggest destroyer of retail returns is buying after rallies and stopping after crashes. **SIP is a behavioral device first, a math device second.** SWP gives systematic withdrawal income, typically for retirement. STP parks a lumpsum in a liquid fund and drips it into equity monthly.

**Thresholds / zones:** None stated beyond the ~3pm same-day NAV cutoff.

**The trap:** Believing SIP mathematically beats lumpsum in general, or treating an open-end fund as illiquid. At worst you pay an exit load — you're never unable to sell.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- SIP = automated, emotion-free averaging; a behavioral fix, not a math trick
- Retail's real failure mode: buying after rallies, selling after crashes
- STP = park a lumpsum, drip into equity, reducing entry-timing risk
- SWP = systematic retirement income
- Open-end funds transact at that day's NAV; exit load is the only friction
