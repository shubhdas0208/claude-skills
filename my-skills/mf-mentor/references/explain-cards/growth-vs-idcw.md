# Growth vs IDCW

**What it is:** Two options on the same fund. Growth: profits stay in the fund and NAV compounds; you realize gains only on sale. IDCW ("Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal," the renamed "dividend"): the fund periodically pays out money and NAV drops by that amount.

**How to read it:** IDCW's name is honest — the payout is partly your own capital handed back, not a bonus. It's taxed at your slab rate immediately, worse than capital gains for most earners, and it destroys compounding. Growth lets you control timing and pay capital-gains rates instead.

**Thresholds / zones:** IDCW payouts carry 10% TDS above ₹10,000/year.

**The trap:** Reading an IDCW payout as free extra income, or seeing NAV drop after a payout and thinking something's wrong. Both misread a partial return of your own capital, taxed immediately at slab.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- NAV drop after IDCW = capital returned, not a loss or a bonus
- IDCW taxed at slab now vs Growth's capital-gains-on-your-schedule
- 10% TDS above ₹10,000/yr on IDCW
- Default, near-universal: Direct + Growth
- SWP from Growth is usually more tax-efficient than choosing IDCW for cash flow
