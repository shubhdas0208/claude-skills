# Cash Levels

**What it is:** The % of an equity fund's portfolio held in cash rather than invested in securities.

**How to read it:** Equity funds normally hold 1–5% cash. Persistent 10%+ cash (a PPFAS signature) signals deliberate defensiveness / dry powder — it explains a low beta, cushions crashes, and drags returns in melt-ups. Separately, a sudden cash spike in a fund normally near-fully invested signals the manager has turned bearish or is bracing for redemptions — a more reactive, different signal than a fund's steady structural buffer.

**Thresholds / zones:** Normal range: 1–5%. Persistent 10%+ = deliberate structural defensiveness (PPFAS example).

**The trap:** Reading every cash level the same way — a steady, structural 10%+ (a stated part of the fund's personality) is very different from a sudden spike in a fund normally near-fully invested. The first is a style choice; the second is reactive and worth investigating.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Normal equity fund cash range is 1–5%
- Persistent 10%+ is a deliberate defensive/dry-powder stance (PPFAS example)
- Structural high cash explains lower beta, crash cushioning, and melt-up drag together
- A sudden cash spike in an otherwise fully-invested fund is different: bearish call or redemption bracing
- Cash level connects directly to the beta/personality read from Chapter 4
