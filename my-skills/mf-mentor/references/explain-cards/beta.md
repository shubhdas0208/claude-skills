# Beta — Sensitivity to the Market

**What it is:** The fund's typical response to its benchmark's moves. Beta 1 = moves with the market. Beta 1.2 = amplifies (market +10% → fund ~+12%; −10% → ~−12%). Beta 0.6 = damped (market −10% → fund ~−6%). NOT a quality score — a *personality* score, aggression vs defensiveness.

**How to read it:** A low-beta fund (e.g., 0.6, from cash + foreign holdings) and a near-market fund (e.g., 0.91) are neither "better" — they shine in different weather; the low-beta fund falls proportionally less in a crash and lags in a melt-up. Beta is only meaningful when the fund actually tracks its benchmark — check R² first. Beta creeping up over time means the manager is getting more aggressive: fine if intended, a flag if it contradicts the fund's stated character.

**Thresholds / zones:** Per §5.7 — weak if beta contradicts the fund's mandate; acceptable/typical is 0.8–1.1; strong is whatever matches the fund's stated personality.

**The trap:** Reading high beta as "risky/bad" and low beta as "safe/good" — beta is a directionality trait, not a quality score, and unreliable without R² support.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- States beta measures amplification/damping vs benchmark, not quality
- Gives a directional read: expected behavior in up vs down markets
- Checks R² before trusting the beta figure
- Reads a beta trend as a shift in manager aggression
- Judges beta against the fund's mandate, not a universal "good" number
