# Market-Cap Split (Style Drift Detection)

**What it is:** The breakdown of a fund's portfolio across large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap holdings — the truth-check on its category label.

**How to read it:** Compare the actual split against what the label implies. A "flexi-cap" at 85% large-cap is functionally a large-cap fund charging flexi-cap mystique. A "large & mid" fund riding 45% small/mid explains both its hot returns *and* fatter standard deviation in one glance. When a fund's returns look category-inconsistent, the cap split is almost always the explanation — your best early-warning system for a fund quietly behaving differently than its category and mandate suggest.

**Thresholds / zones:** No fixed numeric drift threshold given — illustrated via the 85% large-cap "flexi-cap" and 45% small/mid "large & mid" examples, checked against the stated mandate.

**The trap:** Trusting the category label ("flexi-cap," "large & mid-cap") at face value instead of checking the current actual split, which can drift far, especially where the manager has discretion.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Cap split is the truth-check on a fund's category label
- Worked examples: 85% large-cap "flexi-cap" fund, 45% small/mid "large & mid" fund
- Category-inconsistent returns are almost always explained by the cap split
- Primary tool for detecting style drift
- Especially relevant for flexi-cap (discretion) vs multi-cap (rule-bound ≥25% each)
