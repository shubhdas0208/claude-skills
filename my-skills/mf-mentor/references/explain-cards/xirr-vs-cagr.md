# XIRR vs CAGR

**What it is:** XIRR is CAGR's cousin for *multiple* cash flows — it's how your own SIP returns are computed, since each installment compounds for a different length of time.

**How to read it:** Evaluating a fund → CAGR is the fund's number (a single lumpsum, start to end). Evaluating your own portfolio → XIRR is your number (many installments, each with its own holding period). A fund's 5-yr CAGR and your 5-yr SIP XIRR in that fund will differ — both correct, since they answer different questions: "how did the fund do?" (CAGR) vs "how did my timing pattern do?" (XIRR).

**Thresholds / zones:** None given.

**The trap:** Expecting your SIP's XIRR to match the fund's published CAGR, and treating any gap as an error rather than as the natural consequence of comparing a single-flow number to a multi-flow one.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- XIRR handles multiple cash flows with different holding periods; CAGR handles a single start-to-end flow
- CAGR = the fund's number; XIRR = your (the investor's) number
- A fund's CAGR and your SIP's XIRR in that fund can legitimately differ
- The gap between them reflects your timing/entry pattern, not a fund error
- Both numbers are simultaneously correct — they answer different questions
