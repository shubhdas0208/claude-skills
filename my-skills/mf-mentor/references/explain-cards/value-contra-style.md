# Value & Contra: Style Funds Are Lumpy

**What it is:** Value funds (≥80% [2026] mandate) buy statistically cheap/unloved stocks. Contra funds (≥80% [2026] mandate) buy against sentiment. The two overlap heavily — apps often label both "Value Oriented." [2026] AMCs may run both if overlap stays ≤50%.

**How to read it:** These are **style bets**. Returns come in lumps: long stretches of underperformance in momentum markets, then violent outperformance when cheap stocks re-rate. Judging on a 1-year return is a category error — evaluate across a full cycle (5–7+ years), and hold as a *diversifier* alongside growth-style funds, not a replacement.

**Thresholds / zones:** ≥80% [2026] mandate minimum. Full-cycle window: 5–7+ years. [2026] dual Value+Contra overlap cap: ≤50%.

**The trap:** Dropping a style fund after a lagging year, or chasing it after one hot year — both mistake a point in the cycle for the fund's identity. Its worst-looking stretch is often right before its turn.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Style returns are lumpy by design, not by flaw
- Judge across a full cycle (5–7+ years), never a single year
- Hold as a diversifier alongside growth-tilted funds, not a substitute
- Value and Contra overlap heavily despite separate labels
- A value fund lagging in a momentum market is doing its job, not failing
