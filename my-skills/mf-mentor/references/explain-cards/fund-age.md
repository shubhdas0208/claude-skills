# Fund Age

**What it is:** Time since the fund launched. In the handbook's five-fund comparison, all five are ~13 years old. Age is the size of the fund's *evidence base*.

**How to read it:** Under 3 years, the fund shows dashes in a screener's 3Y/5Y columns — statistically it barely exists, and you'd be buying a story, an AMC brand, or a manager's prior record (sometimes legitimate, but know that's your bet). Above 7–10 years, the fund has lived through at least one full fear-greed cycle. How it behaved in stress years is the most valuable page of its history — check calendar-year returns for crash/boom years, since trailing tables hide crisis behavior inside averages.

**Thresholds / zones:** < 3 years = essentially unproven. > 7–10 years = has seen a full cycle.

**The trap:** A 13-year fund whose manager arrived 18 months ago has 13 years of *brand* history and 18 months of *relevant* history — check manager tenure separately.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Fund age = size of the evidence base, not a quality signal alone
- <3 years means no meaningful 3Y/5Y stats — "no data" is itself data
- >7–10 years means the fund has seen a full fear-greed cycle
- Check crisis-year (2020, 2022) calendar returns specifically
- Long fund age can hide a much shorter current-manager tenure
