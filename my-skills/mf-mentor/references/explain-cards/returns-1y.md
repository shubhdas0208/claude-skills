# 1-Year Trailing Return

**What it is:** The trailing return measured back one year from today. Per §3.1's convention, periods of 1 year and beyond are shown annualized (CAGR). The handbook describes what it measures as "one market phase."

**How to read it:** Legitimate uses are screening for disasters and comparing a fund against its category over the same window. The illegitimate use is fund selection — the handbook is blunt: "one year is one coin flip." A fund can top the 1-year table purely because its category or style was in favor that year, not because of manager skill.

**Thresholds / zones:** None given.

**The trap:** Mistaking a recent style tailwind for quality. The handbook's own worked example: a fund was best over 1 year while worst-rated on other metrics, because a recent tailwind (mid-caps holding up) favored it — "not proof of quality."

**Strong answer mentions:**
- 1-year is one market phase, effectively one coin flip
- Legitimate use: disaster screening and same-window category comparison
- Illegitimate use: picking a fund based on 1-year rank
- A top 1-year return can reflect a style/category tailwind, not manager skill
- Must be read within category, same window, not as a standalone score
