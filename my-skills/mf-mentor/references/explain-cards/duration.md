# Duration (Modified/Macaulay)

**What it is:** Average maturity and Modified/Macaulay duration measure a debt fund's interest-rate sensitivity, in years. Duration (in years) ≈ % NAV move per 1% rate move.

**How to read it:** Match duration to your horizon — SEBI's debt category grid is essentially a duration ladder, so pick the rung that matches your goal date rather than picking on yield alone. A duration-6 fund gains/loses roughly 6% per 1% rate move, so a wrong duration for your timeline turns "safe" debt into a rate-cycle gamble.

**Thresholds / zones:** Duration-6 fund ≈ 6% NAV move per 1% rate move. Parking 6-month money in a duration-5 gilt fund is the handbook's explicit example of a horizon/duration mismatch.

**The trap:** Assuming any debt fund is inherently "safe" without checking its duration against your timeline. A fund can hold zero credit risk (gilt) and still be highly volatile purely from rate sensitivity.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Duration in years ≈ % NAV move per 1% rate move
- Match the fund's duration to your specific horizon, not just its category name
- SEBI's debt category grid is effectively a pre-built duration ladder
- The 6-month-money-in-duration-5-gilt-fund mismatch example
- Duration is Axis 1 of the two-axis debt model, independent of credit risk
