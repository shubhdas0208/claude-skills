# Star Ratings (Morningstar / Value Research)

**What it is:** Morningstar rates funds 1–5★ within category, scoring 3/5/10-yr risk-adjusted returns (MRAR, penalizing downside variation; loads/expenses matter), then force-fitting a bell curve: top 10%=5★, next 22.5%=4★, middle 35%=3★, next 22.5%=2★, bottom 10%=1★. VR Rating uses the same logic and limits.

**How to read it:** A 5★ is a relative, backward-looking percentile, recomputed monthly — NOT a buy recommendation, forecast, or comparable across categories (a 5★ credit-risk fund still lends to shaky borrowers). Predictive power is weak: ratings modestly predict expenses/survivorship more than outperformance. Expected pattern: 5★ after a best stretch → inflows → mean-reversion drags to 3★. Use as a *filter* to shrink a list, never a verdict. Two raters agreeing (VR and Morningstar both 5★) means slightly more than either alone.

**Thresholds / zones:** Bands: top 10%=5★, next 22.5%=4★, middle 35%=3★, next 22.5%=2★, bottom 10%=1★. Screening floor: 3★+ (a floor, not a ranking).

**The trap:** Treating a 5★ as a forecast right after a hot stretch — exactly when mean-reversion is most likely.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Relative-within-category percentile, recomputed monthly, not a forecast
- Bell-curve bands: 10/22.5/35/22.5/10 across 5★–1★
- Weak predictive power for outperformance; predicts expenses/survivorship better
- Use as a coarse filter (e.g., 3★+ floor), not to pick a winner
- Watch "5★ after a hot run → inflows → reversion to 3★"
