# Debt Funds: The Two-Axis Model

**What it is:** Debt funds confuse people because there are ~16 categories. They stop being confusing once you see every fund as a position on two axes: **Duration** (rate risk) and **Credit quality** (default risk).

**How to read it:** Axis 1 — Duration: rate rises push bond prices down; longer remaining life means harder price swings per 1% rate move. A duration-6 fund gains/loses ~6% per 1% rate move. Axis 2 — Credit: who the fund lent to — Government (near-zero default risk) → AAA → AA → A and below (yield compensating default risk).

**Thresholds / zones:** Ladder: Overnight (1-day) · Liquid (≤91-day) · Ultra Short (~3–12mo) · Short Duration (1–3yr) · Corporate Bond (≥80% AA+) · Gilt (≥80% govt) · Medium/Long (3–4/4–7/7+ yr) · Credit Risk (≥65% AA & below) · Floater (≥65% floating).

**The trap:** Assuming Gilt is "safe" because government-backed. Zero credit risk, but **high** rate risk — a bet on falling rates, not a safe fund.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Every debt fund = position on duration axis + credit axis
- Duration in years ≈ % NAV move per 1% rate move
- Credit ladder: Sovereign → AAA → AA → A-and-below
- Gilt = zero credit risk, high rate risk, not "safe"
- Credit Risk category caused the 2019–20 blowups — skip without a reason
