# Alpha — The Number Everyone Wants

**What it is:** The return a manager added beyond what the fund's market exposure (beta) alone would have produced: Alpha = Fund return − [Risk-free rate + Beta × (Benchmark return − Risk-free rate)]. Worked example: HDFC Flexi alpha 5.46 → after accounting for its 0.82 beta, the manager added ~5.5%/yr over three years; peers in the same set showed 4.07 and 2.09.

**How to read it:** Alpha is the only number here that even attempts to isolate skill — positive alpha, sustained across market phases and long windows, is the closest thing to evidence a manager earns their fee. An index fund's alpha ≈ −(expense ratio) by construction. High and stable beats very high and episodic.

**Thresholds / zones:** 3–5 sustained over 5+ years is exceptional; a 12 that appeared in one hot year is a coin flip, not skill. §5.7 bands: weak <0, acceptable 0–3, strong 3+ sustained.

**The trap:** Three caveats — (1) inherits beta's low-R² unreliability; (2) benchmark-relative, so a wrong/easy benchmark inflates it; (3) alpha decays — past alpha weakly predicts future alpha, and AUM growth is a known alpha-killer.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- States the CAPM-style formula: alpha isolates skill from market exposure
- Distinguishes high-and-stable from very-high-and-episodic alpha
- Checks R² before trusting the alpha figure
- Names benchmark choice as a source of inflated alpha
- Flags alpha decay and AUM growth as reasons past alpha won't persist
