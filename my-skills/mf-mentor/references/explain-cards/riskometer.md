# The Riskometer — Read It Once, Then Mostly Ignore It

**What it is:** The SEBI-mandated dial with six levels — Low → Low to Moderate → Moderate → Moderately High → High → Very High — color-coded green→red since Dec 2024, recomputed monthly from the fund's actual portfolio.

**How to read it:** Nearly every equity fund shows "Very High," identically. That uniformity is the lesson: the riskometer is a category-level disclosure, not a fund-level discriminator. It won't tell you which specific fund in a category is more disciplined or aggressive than another.

**Thresholds / zones:** Six levels, in order: Low, Low to Moderate, Moderate, Moderately High, High, Very High.

**The trap:** Using the riskometer to rank or compare funds within a category — nearly every equity fund lands on the same level, so it tells you almost nothing about which fund to pick.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Names the six levels in order
- States it's a category-level disclosure, not a fund-level discriminator
- Legitimate use 1: checking a fund's level matches its label (e.g., a "debt" fund showing Moderately High signals hidden credit/duration risk)
- Legitimate use 2: noticing a *change* over time
- Reads a riskometer upgrade as a regulatory nudge that the portfolio got riskier
