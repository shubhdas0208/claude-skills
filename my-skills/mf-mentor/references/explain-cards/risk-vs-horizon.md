# Risk vs Horizon: What "Risk" Actually Means

**What it is:** Apps measure risk as volatility — how much returns wobble around their average. Your real risk is not having the money you need, when you need it. The two connect through time.

**How to read it:** Over months, volatility IS your risk — a dip the week before you need the money is a permanent loss. Over a decade, volatility mostly washes out while compounding works; the bigger risk becomes being *out* of equity (inflation quietly eating a "safe" 6% return at 5–6% inflation). Volatility is only *rented* risk when your horizon is long and behavior steady; it becomes *owned* risk when you're forced — financially or emotionally — to sell in a dip.

**Thresholds / zones:** None given — a framework, not a scored metric. The same fund is dangerous for a 1-year goal and sensible for a 15-year one.

**The trap:** Treating volatility as a fixed, universal danger instead of asking "risk to what, over what horizon."

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Distinguishes volatility (what's measured) from actual risk (not having money when needed)
- Names the horizon effect: short-term dips can be permanent losses; long-term ones wash out
- Identifies the opposite risk of "safe" assets over long horizons — inflation erosion
- Uses "rented vs owned" risk to explain when volatility turns dangerous
- Connects risk tolerance to behavior (forced selling), not just numbers
