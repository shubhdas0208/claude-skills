# R-Squared — Whether Beta Can Be Trusted

**What it is:** R² (0–100) measures how much of the fund's movement its benchmark explains. It's the honest companion stat to beta, introduced because beta is only meaningful when the fund actually tracks its benchmark.

**How to read it:** High R² means beta and alpha are trustworthy signals of market sensitivity and skill. Low R² means the fund marches to its own drummer — true for funds with heavy cash/foreign allocations, international funds, and focused funds — and its beta and alpha readings become statistically mushy. Most Indian apps hide R²; Morningstar's site shows it.

**Thresholds / zones:** R² > ~80 → beta/alpha trustworthy. R² < ~70 → beta/alpha mushy; treat with caution.

**The trap:** Taking a fund's beta or alpha at face value without checking R² first. A low-R² fund's "impressive" alpha or "defensive" beta may just be statistical noise from a portfolio that doesn't resemble its benchmark.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Defines R² as the fraction of fund movement explained by the benchmark
- Names the ~80 / ~70 trustworthy-vs-mushy thresholds
- Applies it as a prerequisite check before trusting beta or alpha
- Recognizes low-R² funds (heavy cash/foreign tilt, international, focused) as expected exceptions, not errors
- Notes R² is often hidden on Indian apps, shown on Morningstar
