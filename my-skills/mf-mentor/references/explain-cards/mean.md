# Mean — The Anchor for Everything Else

**What it is:** The fund's annualized average return over the ~3-year risk-ratio window (computed from monthly returns, annualized). It's the "reward" leg that every ratio in Chapter 5 — Sharpe, Sortino, Treynor, Alpha — divides or adjusts.

**How to read it:** Mean ≠ 3-yr CAGR exactly — arithmetic-vs-geometric averaging and window differences mean the two tables can disagree slightly; don't be alarmed. Alone, mean tells you almost nothing; it's informative only once paired with a risk measure (SD, beta) to see what it cost to earn.

**Thresholds / zones:** None given — mean has no "good" or "bad" level in isolation; it's always read together with SD, beta, or the risk-free rate via Chapter 5's ratios.

**The trap:** Comparing raw means across funds — or across categories — without asking what risk produced them. That's exactly the mistake risk-adjusted ratios exist to correct.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Identifies mean as the "reward" input, not a standalone verdict
- Notes it's annualized over the ~3-year ratio window, distinct from 3-yr CAGR
- Refuses to rank funds on mean alone without pairing it to SD/beta
- Understands mean feeds directly into Sharpe, Sortino, Treynor, and Alpha
