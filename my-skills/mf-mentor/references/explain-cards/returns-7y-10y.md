# 7-Year and 10-Year Trailing Returns

**What it is:** Annualized (CAGR, per §3.1) returns over the longest common windows on the screener — "full cycle(s), manager eras," per the handbook.

**How to read it:** Legit use: judging consistency and crash behavior — "the honest test." Illegit use: overweighting ancient history under a departed manager. The handbook's worked example notes 3-yr and 7-yr columns can cluster tightly (e.g., 18–21% at 7-yr), signaling these are decent funds where category and market conditions did most of the work — and a leader here is "worth investigating why," not "worth buying on sight."

**Thresholds / zones:** None given.

**The trap:** Assuming a strong 7/10-year number reflects the fund as it exists today, when much of that track record may belong to a manager (or a fund era) that's no longer in place.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- 7/10-year spans full market cycles, not just one phase
- Legitimate use: judging consistency and behavior through stress, not just the return level
- Illegitimate use: crediting a long track record to today's manager without checking
- A strong long-window leader is worth investigating why, not buying on sight
- Tight clustering across funds at this window often reflects category/market conditions, not differentiated skill
