# YTM (Yield to Maturity)

**What it is:** The weighted average yield of everything a debt fund holds, if held to maturity.

**How to read it:** First-principles anchor: **YTM − expense ratio ≈ your expected 1-year return**, if rates and credit stay put. No mystery in debt returns — a short-duration fund with YTM 7.4% and TER 0.4% tells you to expect ~7%. An *outlier* YTM is the loudest warning available: peers at 7%, this fund at 8.5% means it's lending to riskier borrowers for that extra 1.5%.

**Thresholds / zones:** Worked example: YTM 7.4% − TER 0.4% ≈ 7% expected return.

**The trap:** Reading a higher YTM as a better fund. In debt, unusually high yields are a **risk signal, not a skill signal** — the extra comes from longer duration (a rate bet) or lower credit quality (a default bet). Yield is the price of risk, never a free lunch.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- YTM − TER ≈ expected 1-yr return, assuming rates/credit stay put
- No mystery in debt returns — the factsheet basically tells you the answer
- An outlier-high YTM vs peers is a red flag, not a reason to buy
- The extra yield is paid for by longer duration or weaker credit
- Check the credit-quality table when YTM looks unusually high
