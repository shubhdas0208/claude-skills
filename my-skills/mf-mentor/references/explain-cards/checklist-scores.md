# Moneycontrol Checklist Score

**What it is:** A composite score /100 across four pillars of 25 each — Performance, Portfolio, Operations, Risk — with verdict chips (GOOD/AVERAGE RETURNS, HIGH/MEDIUM/WEAK RANK). Performance grades returns vs peers; Portfolio grades quality/valuation/concentration; Operations grades expenses, turnover, size, age; Risk grades volatility and risk-adjusted stats. Sub-weights are proprietary.

**How to read it:** The disaggregated row is far more useful than the total. Worked example: Bank of India Small Cap scored Performance 15/25 but Portfolio 6/25 ("WEAK RANK") and Operations 20/25 — a "56/100" hides that it's simultaneously a good performer, a questionable portfolio, and well-run. Use a weak pillar as a pointer to investigate. Scores compress judgments using someone else's weights, and different systems (checklist, AUM order, star ratings) can disagree on the same funds — proof these are opinions with methodologies.

**Thresholds / zones:** No fixed cutoff given — illustrated only via the Bank of India Small Cap example (15/25, 6/25, 20/25).

**The trap:** Reading only the total ("56/100") as a single verdict instead of opening the four pillars.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Four pillars of 25 each: Performance, Portfolio, Operations, Risk
- Total score hides pillar disagreement — always disaggregate
- A weak pillar points to investigate, not a final verdict
- Sub-weights are proprietary; don't assume how the total is built
- Different rating systems disagreeing shows methodology-dependence
