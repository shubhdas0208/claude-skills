# Day Change %

**What it is:** The one-day percentage move in NAV — the number your screener shows as, e.g., −1.2%, −0.6%.

**How to read it:** It is useful only as a liveness check: "small-cap funds all fell ~1% today, the market fell." It confirms the fund is behaving like its category on a given day — nothing more. The handbook's period-by-period table for trailing returns treats 1-Day the same way: what it actually measures is yesterday's NAV move, its legitimate use is liveness/sanity-checking, and its illegitimate use is *any decision at all*.

**Thresholds / zones:** None given.

**The trap:** Treating day change as a selection criterion — reading a fund's daily move as information about fund quality. It is never that. A day's move is ~100% market noise; it tells you about the market, not the manager.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Day change is a liveness/sanity check only — does the fund track its category on a given day
- Legitimate use: noticing the fund reacted to the market as expected
- Illegitimate use: making any investment decision from it
- It reflects the market's mood that day, not fund skill
