# AUM / Net Assets

**What it is:** The fund's total pool size in ₹ Cr — e.g., PPFAS ₹1,43,388 Cr, Nippon Small Cap ₹78,407 Cr, Bank of India Small Cap ₹2,572 Cr.

**How to read it:** Size cuts both ways, and category decides which way. Too small (< ~₹500 Cr, rough) raises viability, higher-expense, and key-person risk. Comfort zone: ~₹1,000–20,000 Cr for active equity. On the "too big" side: in large-cap/liquid, size is nearly free — the market absorbs billions. In small/mid-cap, size is the enemy: a giant fund moving 2% of its book must trade illiquid stocks, forcing 150+ names or up-cap drift. Watch flows too: AUM growing faster than NAV = heavy inflows (mildly negative for future alpha); AUM shrinking while NAV rises = exodus, ask why.

**Thresholds / zones:** Caution below ~₹500 Cr. Comfort zone ~₹1,000–20,000 Cr for active equity.

**The trap:** Admiring a giant small-cap fund's past returns — earned by a smaller, nimbler fund that no longer exists.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- AUM's meaning is category-dependent, not universally good/bad
- Large-cap/liquid absorbs huge AUM cheaply; small/mid-cap doesn't
- Comfort zone ~₹1,000–20,000 Cr; caution below ~₹500 Cr
- A large small-cap fund's future can't repeat the smaller past that earned its record
- AUM vs NAV divergence signals inflow/outflow worth investigating
