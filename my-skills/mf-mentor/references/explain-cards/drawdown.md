# Drawdown — The Risk Number Your Gut Actually Feels

**What it is:** Maximum drawdown = worst peak-to-trough fall. Not in standard trailing-return tables, but it belongs in the toolkit because SD abstracts pain while drawdown states it plainly — "this fund once fell 58% and took 3 years to reclaim its peak" reads differently than "SD 25."

**How to read it:** Before buying any equity fund, find (via a chart, or by eyeballing the NAV graph across stress years like 2020 and 2022) how far it fell and how long it took to recover. Then ask the only honest question in investing: would I have kept my SIP running through that? The answer reveals your true risk tolerance better than any questionnaire.

**Thresholds / zones:** None given — drawdown is a per-fund, look-it-up exercise, not a scored threshold table.

**The trap:** Judging risk tolerance from a questionnaire or SD alone, instead of confronting the actual worst historical fall and honestly asking whether you'd have stayed invested through it.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Defines drawdown as worst peak-to-trough fall, distinct from SD
- Explains why it matters even though SD exists (SD abstracts, drawdown is felt)
- Names the check: how far it fell, how long to recover
- Poses the behavioral test — would I have kept the SIP running
- Looks at actual stress years (2020, 2022), not a summary stat
