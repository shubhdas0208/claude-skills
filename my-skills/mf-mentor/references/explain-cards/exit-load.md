# Exit Load

**What it is:** A % penalty for redeeming within a stated window, shown as "Exit Load (Days)" — e.g., PPFAS **2.00 (365)**: redeem within 365 days, pay 2%; free after. SBI Contra **0.25 (30)**: 0.25% if within 30 days.

**How to read it:** It discourages hot money — short-term flows force the manager to hold cash or trade unnecessarily, taxing loyal unitholders. A stiff load on an equity fund is pro-long-term-investor, not anti-you; PPFAS's 2%/1-year load signals its intended clientele. Check only that the window is shorter than your intended holding period — holding 5+ years makes any equity exit load irrelevant. For parking money it's decisive: liquid funds have graded loads only up to 7 days.

**Thresholds / zones:** PPFAS 2.00% within 365 days; SBI Contra 0.25% within 30 days. Liquid funds: graded loads only up to 7 days.

**The trap:** Each SIP installment has its own clock — a 10-month-old SIP position can still have recent installments inside the load window.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Decodes as "X% if redeemed within Y days"
- Protects long-term unitholders from costs hot money imposes
- Only matters if your holding period is shorter than the load window
- Decisive for parking short-term cash — avoid long windows there
- Each SIP installment has its own load clock (FIFO, per-unit)
