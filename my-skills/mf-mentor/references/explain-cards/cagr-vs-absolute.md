# CAGR vs Absolute Return

**What it is:** **Absolute return = (End − Start) / Start** — the total move, fine for periods ≤1 year. **CAGR = (End ÷ Start)^(1 ÷ years) − 1** — the constant yearly rate that would take Start to End. Convention: under 1 year shown absolute; 1 year and beyond shown annualized (CAGR).

**How to read it:** "3Yr CAGR 27.7%" means ₹100 became ~₹208 over three years — not a 27.7% total move. Two properties: (1) CAGR smooths violently — a fund that went +60%, −30%, +25% has a 3-yr CAGR of ~11.9%, hiding a gut-wrenching ride; it shows the destination, never the journey. (2) CAGR is point-to-point — depends only on start/end dates, so moving the window by a month can change the number dramatically, generating most trailing-table traps.

**Thresholds / zones:** None given.

**The trap:** Reading a multi-year CAGR as if it describes a smooth, steady climb, or as a total percentage gain rather than an annual rate.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Absolute = (End−Start)/Start; used for periods ≤1 year
- CAGR = (End/Start)^(1/years) − 1; used for periods ≥1 year
- A CAGR must be compounded, not read as a total return
- CAGR smooths volatility — hides the journey, shows only the destination
- CAGR is point-to-point — sensitive to exact start/end dates
