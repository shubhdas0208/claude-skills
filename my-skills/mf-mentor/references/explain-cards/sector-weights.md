# Sector Weights vs Benchmark (incl. Closet Indexing)

**What it is:** How a fund's sector allocation compares to its benchmark index's sector allocation.

**How to read it:** The right question is never "is 30% financials bad" in isolation (financials are ~30% of most Indian indices) — it's "where does the fund differ from its index, and is that where its alpha comes from?" A fund overweight autos and underweight IT is making implicit sector calls, and its future depends on them playing out. If sector weights ≈ benchmark, alpha ≈ 0, and R² ≈ 95+, you've found a **closet indexer**: index-like performance at active-fund prices. The handbook's verdict: "Sell."

**Thresholds / zones:** Closet-indexer signature (all three together): sector weights ≈ benchmark, alpha ≈ 0, R² ≈ 95+.

**The trap:** Reacting to a large sector weight in isolation ("30% in financials looks risky") without comparing it to the benchmark's own sector composition first.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Judge sector weight by deviation from the benchmark, not the absolute number
- Overweight/underweight sectors are implicit bets whose future depends on those calls
- Closet indexer signature: sector weights ≈ benchmark + alpha ≈ 0 + R² ≈ 95+
- A closet indexer is index performance at active prices — the handbook says sell
- Ties alpha's source (§5.5) to whether sector deviations are actually paying off
