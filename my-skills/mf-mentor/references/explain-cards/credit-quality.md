# Credit Quality & Issuer Concentration

**What it is:** The credit-quality profile is the factsheet table showing % in Sovereign / AAA / AA / A & below. Issuer concentration is the % lent to any single issuer.

**How to read it:** Default beginner posture: **≥90% in sovereign + AAA** (liquid, money market, corporate bond, banking & PSU, gilt categories). One issuer at 8–10% of a credit-tilted book is one default away from a visible NAV hit — prefer granular books.

**Thresholds / zones:** ≥90% sovereign+AAA = beginner default posture. 8–10% in a single issuer = concentration flag. (Corporate Bond funds require ≥80% AA+; Credit Risk funds hold ≥65% AA and below.)

**The trap:** Assuming credit risk is invisible. The 2019–20 credit crisis (defaults → side-pocketed schemes → frozen redemptions) happened almost entirely in funds whose credit tables telegraphed the risk for months in public disclosures nobody read.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- The credit table: Sovereign / AAA / AA / A-and-below, read from the factsheet
- ≥90% sovereign+AAA as the beginner default posture
- Issuer concentration risk: 8–10% in one name is a real flag
- The 2019–20 crisis lesson — warning signs were public and ignored
- Credit Risk category = junk-adjacent lending for extra yield; skip without a specific reason
