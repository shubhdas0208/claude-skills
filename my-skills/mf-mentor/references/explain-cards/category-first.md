# Category First, Fund Second

**What it is:** SEBI's post-2017 categorization forces every scheme into one category with defined portfolio rules. Category is the most load-bearing field — it sets legal constraints, risk band, benchmark, and peer group.

**How to read it:** Two iron rules. (1) Every metric is only meaningful *within* a category — never compare across categories; compare against category peers and the category average. (2) Category choice matters more than fund choice — decide the category from your goal first, then pick the fund.

**Thresholds / zones:** Best-vs-worst large-cap fund might differ by 3–4%/yr. Small-cap versus liquid at the wrong time can differ by 40% in a year.

**The trap:** Judging a Sharpe of 0.85 as universally "good," or 25% SD as universally "alarming." Sharpe 0.85 is good for a flexi-cap, meaningless vs a liquid fund; 25% SD is normal for small-cap, alarming for large-cap.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Category = legal constraints + risk band + benchmark + peer group, one label
- Never compare a metric across categories — only within category, same window
- Category choice dwarfs fund choice (3–4%/yr vs up to 40%/yr)
- Decide category from goal/horizon first, fund last
- SEBI's Oct 2017 categorization is why every app shows the same skeleton
