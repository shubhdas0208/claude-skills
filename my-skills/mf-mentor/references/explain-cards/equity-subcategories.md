# The Equity Market-Cap Ladder

**What it is:** SEBI/AMFI rank companies by market cap, re-ranked half-yearly: **Large-cap = top 100. Mid-cap = 101st–250th. Small-cap = 251st onward.** Every equity sub-category is a rule built on this ladder.

**How to read it:** Match the sub-category's rule to what you want: Large-Cap (≥80% large) for stability; Large & Mid-Cap (≥35% large + ≥35% mid) — a forced barbell, more aggressive than pure large-cap; Mid-Cap (≥65% mid) — scaling businesses, deeper crashes, less coverage; Small-Cap (≥65% small) — highest return potential, deepest drawdowns (50%+ in bad cycles), liquidity risk; Multi-Cap (≥75% equity, ≥25% each bucket) — rules-guaranteed diversification; Flexi-Cap (≥65% equity, any mix) — full manager discretion.

**Thresholds / zones:** SEBI minimums as above. Small-cap rewards 7–10 year horizons, punishes tourists.

**The trap:** Treating Large & Mid-Cap as a mild step up from Large-Cap — the forced ≥35% mid sleeve makes it meaningfully more volatile. Assuming "flexi-cap" implies diversification without checking its actual cap split.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Rank-based definitions (100 / 250 / 251+), re-ranked half-yearly
- Each sub-category's SEBI minimum percentage rule
- Large & Mid is a forced, structurally more aggressive barbell
- Small-cap's extra risk is liquidity, not just volatility
- Category rules decide behavior before manager skill enters the picture
