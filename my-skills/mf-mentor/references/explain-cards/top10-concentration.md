# Number of Stocks & Top-10 Concentration

**What it is:** How many stocks a fund holds, and what % of the portfolio the top 10 represent. The handbook contrasts: 25 stocks with top-10 = 55% (focused, conviction fund) vs 120 stocks with top-10 = 25% (diversified, index-hugging profile).

**How to read it:** Neither concentrated nor diversified is inherently wrong — each implies different behavior. Concentrated funds carry higher fund-specific risk, which shows up as gaps between Sharpe and Treynor ratios (§5.4): when they disagree, it's a cue to check the portfolio for concentration. In a concentrated fund, manager calls can make or break the outcome; in a diversified fund, individual stock calls matter far less.

**Thresholds / zones:** Only illustrative examples given (25 stocks/55% top-10 = concentrated; 120 stocks/25% top-10 = diversified) — no universal numeric cutoff.

**The trap:** A fund's concentration jumping sharply without a stated strategy change — a red flag, since a shift should be explainable by a deliberate mandate or manager decision.

**Strong answer mentions:**
- Concentration is not good/bad by itself — implies a different risk-return character
- Concentrated (fewer stocks, higher top-10%) = higher fund-specific risk
- Diversified (many stocks, lower top-10%) = more index-hugging
- Links to Sharpe-vs-Treynor divergence to detect concentration risk
- Red flag: concentration jumping sharply without an explained strategy change
